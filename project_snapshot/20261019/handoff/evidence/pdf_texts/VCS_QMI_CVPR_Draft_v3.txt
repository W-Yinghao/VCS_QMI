Abstract
Learning representations through statistical dependence re-
quires a sample-based objective and a useful implementa-
tion of that objective. We study variational Cauchy–Schwarz
quadratic mutual information (VCS-QMI) under the equal
mixture of a joint distribution and the product of its marginals.
This reference measure makes CS divergence a monotone
transform of a bounded quadratic score. The score has an
exact variational representation using first- and second-order
critic moments, a population optimizer tanh(PMI /2), and a
squared-regression variational gap. We retain this construc-
tion and its fixed-distribution error analysis, then study its use
in visual self-supervised learning. The current implementa-
tion uses a shared encoder and projector, a bounded cosine
critic, separately averaged joint and off-diagonal terms, and
a disclosed stop-gradient on the shifted negative partner. A
multi-view version averages the same score over view pairs.
On a fixed CIFAR-10 development split, the original two-view
MLP implementation reaches 74.34 ± 0.46% frozen-feature
linear accuracy; the current four-view, 200-epoch configura-
tion reaches 84.54 ± 0.16%, and one four-view, 800-epoch
run reaches 86.42%. These are internal selection results,
not official-test or equal-budget superiority claims. The ex-
periments distinguish implementation effects, representation
quality, and training cost; controlled estimator experiments,
broader benchmarks, and matched baseline tuning remain
open.
1 Introduction
Self-supervised visual learning uses relations between observa-
tions to train an encoder without semantic training labels. Aug-
mented views of the same image provide joint observations,
while views from different images provide an off-diagonal
reference. The training criterion is important, but so are the
function class, augmentations, projector, and gradient paths.
Estimating more dependence does not by itself establish a
better visual representation [4, 15].
Neural mutual information estimation makes dependence
trainable from samples. MINE uses a Kullback–Leibler (KL)
variational representation whose Donsker–Varadhan objective
contains an exponential critic moment [ 3]. Different varia-
tional estimators have different bias–variance behavior, espe-
cially at high dependence [10, 12]. This motivates examining
other statistical objectives, without assuming that all KL-based
or contrastive objectives are unstable.
CS divergence compares densities through normalized over-
lap. The reference measure matters because changing it
generally changes density-square integrals. We retain the
collaborator’s equal-mixture construction: for P = PXY and
Q = PXPY , set M = (P + Q)/2. The densities relative to M
sum to two. This identity reduces mixture-reference CS to
a monotone transform of a bounded score S. Completing a
square yields a sample-only quadratic variational objective, an
optimal bounded PMI transform, and an exact regression gap.
The target is not Shannon MI or the classical fixed-reference
CS value. Its relation to triangular discrimination, relative
density-ratio fitting, and balanced relative predictive coding
(RPC) is stated explicitly [14, 18]. The construction and the
sampling analysis remain the theoretical core. We do not
attribute a new divergence or an unrestricted new variational
principle to an already related quadratic functional.
The visual experiments now provide evidence beyond the
initial implementation check. Under one common CIFAR-10
development protocol, changing the critic from a concatena-
tion MLP to a cosine form improves frozen features. With
that critic, a negative-partner stop-gradient and additional in-
dependently augmented views improve the selected configu-
rations further. This stop-gradient retains the score’s forward
value but changes the encoder’s derivative; the distinction
is part of the method, not hidden in an implementation de-
tail. No auxiliary covariance, orthogonality, reconstruction, or
marginal-alignment loss is added.
Contributions and current scope. The manuscript con-
nects three components: the retained mixture-reference CS
formulation and exact variational characterization; a neural es-
timator with an explicit fixed-distribution error decomposition;
and a reproducible SSL study showing how a concrete imple-
mentation can improve visual features. The current evidence
is a development study on 45,000 fitting and 5,000 selection
images, with multiple pretraining seeds for key configurations.

2 Related Work
Neural dependence estimation. MINE provides a neural
estimator through KL variational representations [3]. Nguyen
et al. study divergence estimation through convex risk mini-
mization [8]. Poole et al. analyze variational MI bounds, while
Song and Ermon study their limitations and variance reduction
through SMILE [10, 12]. These works motivate evaluating
bias, variance, and useful learning separately. Since their KL-
based targets differ from S, their numerical outputs are not
compared against the CS target as though they estimated the
same quantity.
CS and relative density ratios. CS-IB uses CS-based pre-
diction and compression terms for regression; conditional CS
compares conditional laws through kernel estimators [21, 22].
CS-Aligner adds marginal-distribution alignment to pairwise
visual–language learning [20]. Our joint-versus-product com-
parison instead measures dependence between two views.
LSMI fits an ordinary density ratio with a squared criterion,
and RuLSIF fits a relative density ratio under a mixture distri-
bution [13, 18]. General variational f -divergence methods and
Fisher GAN provide related quadratic formulations [7, 9]. Re-
lated bounded dependence measures also appear in geometric
mutual information [19].

5.3 Interpreting stability in learning
The estimator avoids exponential critic moments and has
bounded sample terms. These are specific structural properties,
not a universal variance ranking across different dependence
targets. Neither ∇φ Tφ nor the encoder Jacobian is bounded
merely because Tφ ∈ [−1,1]. Gradient behavior in end-to-end
SSL is measured rather than inferred from Eq. (23).
The bounded score can saturate near one, and its CS trans-
form becomes sensitive there. A nearly constant score with
low variance is not necessarily an informative estimator. Like-
wise, stable arithmetic does not establish non-collapse or se-
mantic usefulness. The empirical design therefore measures
dependence resolution, fitted-critic generalization, feature vari-
ation, and frozen linear accuracy separately.

6.2 Data, augmentations, and frozen-feature
evaluation
CIFAR-10’s official training portion is partitioned by image
identity using split seed 20260924: 500 images per class form
selection and the remaining 45,000 form fitting. The saved
manifest is common to all methods. Labels are used for the
stratified split and downstream probes, never as inputs to the
SSL loss or negative selection. Every view of one image stays
in its assigned partition.
Each view uses random resized crop to 32 × 32 with area
scale [0.2,1], aspect ratio [3/4,4/3], bilinear interpolation and
antialiasing; horizontal flip with probability 0.5; brightness/-

tion are off. The pending strong-augmentation run changes
crop and jitter and is not mixed with the default results.
The primary endpoint is a linear classifier on the frozen
512-dimensional h, before projector and critic. Clean images
use only tensor conversion and the same fixed normalization.
A linear layer with bias is fitted on the 45,000 fitting features

20260925. The final probe epoch is reported. No feature

6.3 From the initial implementation to the cur-
rent one
Table 2 reports the accumulated development evidence. The
original MLP version learns useful features, but changing its
capacity did not close its gap to the matched controls. A cosine
critic and then negative-partner stop-gradient improved three-
seed performance. Initial scale α0 = 5 helped the two-view
configuration, while its gain largely overlapped with four-
view training. These changes are observed implementation
effects, not additive proof that each mechanism contributes
independently.
The controls have not yet received the subsequent recipe
search. They are useful fixed reference points, not fully re-
tuned comparators. A same-score balanced RPC implementa-
tion is an identity check; a tuned RPC study is still absent from
the current evidence. We do not infer a general SSL advantage
from selective comparison against the frozen P5 recipes.
6.4 Views, updates, and training horizon
Table 3 separates views, image batch, steps, and encoded-
view exposure. With two views and 800 epochs, the selected
configuration reaches 85.30 ± 0.21% over three seeds. Eight
views and 200 epochs reach 86.28% in one completed run.
Four views and 800 epochs reach 86.42% in one completed
run, with kNN 85.60%. The latter is the largest final linear
value verified in the inspected stage tables; the other two seeds
and larger-horizon runs remain pending.
At the shorter budget, reducing the four-view image batch
from 256 to 128 increases update count and raises linear vali-
dation from 81.88 to 83.19 ± 0.40%. This also changes batch
statistics and the identities contributing per update, so the ef-
fect is not attributed to update count alone. At approximately
the same encoded-view exposure and number of updates
as four views/200 epochs, eight views/100 epochs/ B = 128
reaches 84.60, whereas eight views/100 epochs/ B = 256
reaches 83.40. Thus the earlier conclusion that eight views are
intrinsically worse is not supported.
Long-horizon exploration is currently aimed at achievable
quality, not an efficiency claim. The 86.42% VCS endpoint
uses eight times the encoded views of the two-view 200-epoch
control and is a single seed. It cannot establish superiority over
a tuned control at equal budget. Likewise, a continued rise in
kNN is not an extrapolation of unmeasured linear accuracy.
6.5 Ablations and diagnostics
Disabling negative-partner stop-gradient in the four-view,
B = 256, 100-epoch setting lowers linear validation from
81.88 to 79.66. This is the relevant within-setting compara-
tor; comparing 79.66 directly with the B = 128 three-seed
result would confound the routing change with batch size. At
B = 128, increasing projector output from 128 to 512, widen-
ing the hidden layer to 2048, or using K = 127 yields 82.88,
82.82, and 82.86 respectively, versus the 83.19 mean. These
single-seed ablations do not establish universal irrelevance of
those settings.
Geometry is diagnostic, not the endpoint. Effective rank
is the exponential entropy of normalized eigenvalues of cen-
tered covariance on a fixed 4,096-selection-image subset. In
the inspected diagnostic runs, stronger frozen- h results can
coexist with a more concentrated normalized projector space.
Alignment and uniformity [17] and per-layer probes therefore
remain separate from linear accuracy. Their co-variation does
not by itself prove the mechanism of improvement. A more
expressive critic, output BN, or an added metric matrix did
not consistently improve the tested configurations; rank con-
centration is not equated with the complete absence of useful
information.
The derivative of a pair score with respect to its logit u is
(c − T )(1 − T 2), with c ∈ {−1, +1}. Saturation can attenuate
this factor, but total parameter gradients also contain scale
and network derivatives. Reports of lower positive saturation
under detach are observations at specified checkpoints; they
are not true for every long-horizon endpoint and do not prove
universal gradient stability.
6.6 What remains untested
Controlled Gaussian/mixture/nonlinear-channel studies re-
tain the original plan’s target-specific evaluation: estimate
S against analytic or independent high-precision Monte Carlo
references, and assess other MI estimators against their own
targets. Those results have not been supplied in this repository
snapshot. Formal CIFAR-100/ImageNet-scale experiments,
official-test evaluation, and broader batch/learning-rate com-
parisons against tuned methods are likewise pending.
P41 is a draft, unlaunched control-tuning plan, not a com-
pleted equal-search-budget comparison. Its planned knobs do
not retroactively equalize the approximately 150 exploratory
VCS runs reported by the technical note. Both per-run ex-
posure and cumulative search cost must be disclosed. The
second-application options are maintained in a separate docu-
ment; no candidate is assumed to have run or to improve the
method.
7 Discussion
The fixed-distribution CS construction and the empirical SSL
implementation should not be conflated. The same moment
score can be estimated with a flexible bounded neural critic,
while the selected visual implementation uses a restricted
cosine family and a negative-partner semi-gradient. Better
frozen features under that implementation do not prove exact
estimation of the population supremum or inherit an end-to-
end convergence theorem.
The current results support a useful implementation and
reproducible gains over its initial version. They do not estab-

8 Conclusion
We retain a mixture-reference CS dependence functional with
an exact quadratic variational representation, bounded PMI
optimizer, and squared-regression gap. The updated SSL
study specifies how it is actually implemented: a shared
encoder/projector, bounded cosine critic, off-diagonal sam-
pling, disclosed negative-partner stop-gradient, and view-pair
averaging. The current four-view 200-epoch configuration
reaches 84.54 ± 0.16% frozen linear validation on CIFAR-
10; a completed four-view 800-epoch run reaches 86.42%.
These development results establish useful learning and iden-
tify implementation-sensitive behavior. Statistical superiority,
official-test performance, larger-scale transfer, and a second
application remain to be demonstrated.

D Exact Current SSL Implementation
D.1 What is inherited and what is empirical
The collaborator’s research plan supplies the equal-mixture
reference, the moment objective, its variational identity and
regression gap, the bounded optimal critic, and the neural
parameterization T = tanh f . These are retained in Sec. 3.
The plan does not prescribe the concatenation MLP, ResNet,
projector, cosine function, or negative-partner stop-gradient.
Those are implementation choices evaluated in the repository.
The original SSL starting point is still a valid reported base-
line: two views, K = 1, concatenation MLP, and full input
backpropagation. The current reference configuration is dif-
ferent: four views, K = 8, cosine critic, α0 = 5, and explicit
negative-partner stop-gradient. It is called the current config-
uration, rather than a theoretically necessary or universally
optimal architecture.
D.2 Architecture and tensor layout
Input views are a list of tensors [x1, . . . ,xV ], each of shape
B × 3 × 32 × 32. Concatenation is view-major, producing
V B images. The modified ResNet-18 removes the original
classifier and initial max-pool, and replaces the stem with a
3 × 3 stride-one, padding-one convolution without bias. Its
feature dimension is 512. The reported parameter counts are
11,168,832 for the encoder and 328,832 for the projector.
The projector is a bias-free 512 × 512 linear map, Batch-
Norm1d, ReLU, and a 512 × 128 linear map with bias. No
final BN, dropout, or LayerNorm is present in the current con-
figuration. L2 normalization uses 10−8 as the norm floor. The
cosine critic has only two learned scalar parameters; legacy
Y AML keys such ashidden_dims: [512,512] are not ac-
tive layers when input: cosine .
After projection, view-major chunks recover zv ∈ RB×128.
Every view participates in the common BN forward. The im-
plementation is not V independent forwards with independent
BN statistics.

D.6 Optimization, initialization, and schedules
There is one AdamW optimizer with explicitly separated pa-
rameter groups. The peak learning rate is 10−3; betas are
(0.9,0.999) and epsilon 10−8. Matrix weights in the en-
coder/projector receive decay 10−4. Biases, normalization pa-
rameters, and critic scalars receive zero decay. Critic learning-
rate multiplier is one. There is no gradient clipping or accu-
mulation, and no additional critic-only update in the selected
configuration.
With Nfit = 45,000, drop-last gives SB = ⌊Nfit/B⌋ updates
per epoch. Each horizon has its own warm-up and de-
cay. The schedule is updated each optimizer step, warms
up over 10SB steps, and then follows cosine decay to-
ward 1% of the peak. The exact endpoint convention fol-
lows src/vcs_ssl/schedule.py; it is not changed by this
manuscript. Batch size does not automatically rescale the
learning rate.
Encoder and projector are initialized from the run
seed. Critic initialization uses the separate stream seed *
1000003 + 7919 ; cosine scalar initializations are determin-
istic. The pair generator uses seed + 100003 . Data-loader
and monitoring streams are recorded separately. The primary
precision is FP32; TF32 and compilation are disabled. Data
loading uses four workers, pinned memory on GPU, and non-
persistent workers.
Long runs resume at saved epoch boundaries with the same
intended horizon, configuration, optimizer, and random-state
payload. A migration to another GPU is not a new statistical
seed. The repository records CPU resume tests and GPU
nondeterminism qualifications; this revision did not execute
those server tests or assert cross-hardware bitwise equality.

E.1 Primary frozen linear endpoint
A fresh model copy loads the specified final checkpoint. The
encoder is in evaluation mode and its parameters and Batch-

without L2 normalization or feature standardization for the
primary probe. The head is one linear layer with bias, trained
on fitting-set labels only.
The head optimizer is SGD with learning rate 0.1, momen-
tum 0.9, no weight decay, batch size 256, and 100 epochs. Its
stepwise cosine schedule reaches 0.1% of the initial learning
rate; the fixed seed is 20260925. The final probe epoch is re-

Feature caches are keyed by checkpoint, transform, iden-

E.2 kNN, spectrum, and critic validation
Weighted kNN uses cosine similarities on L2-normalized h,
k = 200, and temperature 0.1, with fitting features as the bank
and selection features as queries. The weighting subtracts the
maximum top-k similarity before exponentiation for numerical
stability. This exponential belongs to the kNN evaluation rule,
not to the VCS training objective.

tion identities, center the features, and form sample covari-

E.3 Monitoring and failures
The trainer evaluates a separate checkpoint copy under inde-

prints are unchanged. Logged quantities include J, the binary
risk, score means and second moments, the fractions with
|T | > 0.95, and module-wise gradient norms. The selected
cosine implementation also records its scale and bias. Check-
point trajectories are not final results from shorter-horizon
schedules.
Failure and stopped-run artifacts are retained by the repos-

manuscript quotes executed results and inspections; it does
