Abstract
Learning representations through statistical dependence re-
quires a sample-based objective with a tractable estimator. We
study variational Cauchy–Schwarz quadratic mutual informa-
tion (VCS-QMI) under the equal mixture of a joint distribu-
tion and the product of its marginals. This reference measure
makes the CS divergence a monotone transform of a bounded
quadratic dependence score. The score admits an exact vari-
ational representation involving only first- and second-order
critic moments. Its population optimizer is tanh(PMI /2), and
its variational gap is exactly a squared regression error. These
identities yield a neural estimator without explicit density
estimation or exponential critic moments. We organize its
statistical analysis around approximation, sampling, and op-
timization errors, and distinguish the bounded training score
from the sensitive logarithmic CS transform. For visual rep-
resentation learning, we instantiate the estimator in a conven-
tional two-view encoder–projector framework with a bounded
pairwise critic and ordinary joint backpropagation. Our eval-
uation protocol separates controlled dependence estimation,
training sensitivity, computational cost, and frozen-feature
quality. It also separates short development runs from multi-
seed benchmark evaluation. Empirical improvements are not
asserted before these experiments are completed.
1 Introduction
Self-supervised visual learning uses relationships between ob-
servations to learn an encoder without semantic training labels.
In a two-view setting, independently sampled augmentations
of the same image provide paired observations, while views
from different images provide a reference for independent
pairing. This construction supports several successful learning
objectives, but the usefulness of the learned representation also
depends on the augmentations, architecture, and projection
head [4, 8, 18]. An improved dependence estimate is therefore
not, by itself, evidence of an improved visual encoder.
Neural mutual information estimation offers a flexible way
to turn dependence into a trainable objective. MINE uses
a variational representation of Kullback–Leibler (KL) diver-
gence whose Donsker–Varadhan form contains an exponential
critic moment [3]. Subsequent analyses show that particular
variational estimators can exhibit difficult bias–variance trade-
offs, especially at high dependence [13, 15]. This motivates
investigating other statistical objectives, without treating KL
divergence or all contrastive losses as intrinsically unstable.
Cauchy–Schwarz (CS) divergence compares density func-
tions through their normalized overlap. Kernel-based CS quan-
tities have been used for information-bottleneck regression
and conditional-distribution comparison [22, 23]. CS-Aligner
also uses CS for visual–language marginal-distribution align-
ment [21]. These applications motivate a sample-based neural
treatment of CS dependence, but two decisions must remain
explicit: which distributions are compared, and relative to
which measure their densities are defined.
We study the equal-mixture reference construction for
P = PXY and Q = PXPY . With M = (P + Q)/2, the two densi-
ties relative to M sum to two. This identity reduces mixture-
reference CS divergence to a monotone transform of a bounded
score S. Completing a square gives an exact variational rep-
resentation of S involving only the first and second moments
of a critic under P and Q. The optimal critic is the bounded
pointwise-mutual-information transform tanh(PMI /2), and
the difference between S and a critic’s objective is its squared
regression error under M.
This route makes the reference measure part of the learning
objective, rather than an implicit choice in density estima-
tion. It does not estimate Shannon MI or the classical fixed-
reference CS value. The bounded score is related to triangular
discrimination, relative density-ratio fitting, and a balanced
instance of relative predictive coding (RPC) [17, 19]. We re-
tain these relationships in the formulation and comparisons.
The paper studies the CS interpretation, the resulting estima-
tor, and its use in visual learning; it does not present every
component of the quadratic objective as a new divergence.
For visual SSL, we use a shared encoder, a projection head,
and a pointwise tanh critic. Real pairs consist of two views
of one image. Off-diagonal pairs are formed by a nonzero
cyclic shift of the second view. All trainable modules receive
gradients from the same quadratic objective in an ordinary
joint update. No separate gradient estimator, teacher network,
marginal-alignment term, or auxiliary anti-collapse loss is

2 Related Work
Neural dependence estimation. MINE provides a neural es-
timator through KL variational representations [3]. Nguyen et
al. study divergence estimation through convex risk minimiza-
tion [11]. Poole et al. analyze variational MI bounds, while
Song and Ermon study their limitations and variance reduction
through SMILE [13, 15]. These works motivate evaluating
bias, variance, and useful learning separately. Since their KL-
based targets differ from S, their numerical outputs are not
compared against the CS target as though they estimated the
same quantity.

CS-Aligner adds marginal-distribution alignment to pairwise
visual–language learning [21]. Our joint-versus-product com-

LSMI fits an ordinary density ratio with a squared criterion,

bution [16, 19]. General variational f -divergence methods and
Fisher GAN provide related quadratic formulations [10, 12].
Related bounded dependence measures also appear in geomet-

4.3 Ordinary joint optimization
The SSL loss is
LVCS(θ , ψ, φ ) =− bJθ ,ψ (Tφ ). (17)
All three modules maximize the same score. There is no
adversarial sign reversal, detached negative representation,
critic warm-up, or extra critic-only update in the pilot. Table 1
describes one step. The raw objective is neither clipped at zero
nor replaced by its logarithmic CS transform. The pilot has no
auxiliary variance, covariance, reconstruction, or alignment
loss; parameter weight decay is reported separately as an
optimizer setting.
The population target contains a supremum over the critic; a
simultaneous finite-capacity update does not solve that supre-
mum at each encoder step. We therefore report the actual fitted-
critic score and evaluate the encoder independently. Ordinary
alternating optimization remains a possible later ablation, not
an unreported change to the current update rule.
4.4 Reporting and cost
Training logs use Jraw and Rbinary, with J = 1 − R checked
numerically. A negative J is possible for a suboptimal critic
and is retained. Evaluation on unseen images estimates the
current fitted critic’s score, not the unknown optimized S.
Any later plug-in CS display would require its own domain
projection and clipping report; the pilot reports no transformed
CS value.
With fixed network sizes and K, the pairwise objective
needs B(1 + K) critic evaluations, rather than a full B2 pair
matrix. Total cost still includes the encoder, projector, critic,
and evaluation. We record actual time, peak memory, pair
counts, and optimizer steps. This computational form alone
is not evidence of a faster or more sample-efficient learning
system.

5.3 Interpreting stability in learning
The estimator avoids exponential critic moments and has
bounded sample terms. These are specific structural properties,
not a universal variance ranking across different dependence
targets. Neither ∇φ Tφ nor the encoder Jacobian is bounded
merely because Tφ ∈ [−1,1]. Gradient behavior in end-to-end
SSL is measured rather than inferred from Eq. (18).
The bounded score can saturate near one, and its CS trans-
form becomes sensitive there. A nearly constant score with
low variance is not necessarily an informative estimator. Like-
wise, stable arithmetic does not establish non-collapse or se-
mantic usefulness. The empirical design therefore measures
dependence resolution, fitted-critic generalization, feature vari-
ation, and frozen linear accuracy separately.

6.1 Questions and experimental levels
We organize the evaluation around four questions. Q1: does
the neural estimator recover its specified dependence target?
Q2: does it remain informative across sample counts and
dependence strengths? Q3: does ordinary end-to-end training
improve frozen visual features? Q4: how do feature quality,
training sensitivity, and cost change with the implementation
budget? These questions distinguish a useful estimator from a
useful representation-learning system.

6.3 CIFAR-10 development pilot
The pilot uses only the official CIFAR-10 training portion [7].
A fixed, stratified image-identity split assigns 45,000 images
to SSL fitting and 5,000 to development selection. Both views
of an image stay in its assigned role. Labels are used only
to construct the fixed split and for downstream evaluation;
the SSL trainer receives views and image identities, not class
labels. The official test split is not read.
Three runs use the same backbone/projector initializa-
tion, augmentations, data roles, and 20-epoch budget: VCS-
QMI , a matched SimCLR implementation, and VICReg
matched-128. The VCS and SimCLR inputs are L2-
normalized, whereas VICReg receives the unnormalized pro-
jector output. This matched 128-dimensional VICReg is a
component-control experiment, not a substitute for a method-
native VICReg baseline. SimCLR uses NT-Xent with tem-
perature 0.2 and its 2B − 2 negative views per anchor; VCS
uses K = 1. Their negative-sampling budgets are explicitly
different.
The VCS run uses one GPU, FP32, image batch B = 256,
seed 0, and AdamW with a base learning rate of 10−3. It
has two epochs of linear learning-rate warm-up followed by
cosine decay to 1% of the base rate. Weight decay is 10−4
on encoder/projector matrix weights and zero on their bias/-
BatchNorm parameters and all critic parameters. There is no
gradient clipping. These are frozen development defaults, not

6.4 Frozen-feature evaluation and diagnostics
The primary representation is the 512-dimensional backbone
output h, before the projector and critic. A separate check-
point copy is set to evaluation mode, with parameters and
BatchNorm buffers frozen. Clean image transforms generate
cached features. The pilot fits a linear classifier on fitting-set
labels and evaluates selection-set labels; the metric is linear
validation top-1, not official test accuracy.
Both the initialization checkpoint and epoch 20 receive
the same probe. The probe uses a fixed 100-epoch SGD
schedule and its final checkpoint, rather than selecting the best
validation epoch. Weighted kNN at epochs 0, 5, 10, and 20
is a secondary diagnostic. Independent evaluation random
streams and model copies prevent monitoring from changing
subsequent training. All probe details are in Appendix E.
In addition to the probe, we log raw J, squared risk, pos-
itive/negative score moments, endpoint-neighborhood frac-
tions, and module-wise gradient norms. On the same fixed
4,096-image subset, we measure the variance and covariance
spectrum of h, the raw projector output, and its normalized
version. Effective rank is computed from the centered covari-
ance eigenvalues. Unit sample norm does not demonstrate
non-collapse. Four selection-set augmentation/pairing repeats
estimate the variability of the current held-out critic score
without refitting the critic. This diagnostic is not a two-sided
confidence interval for S.
Table 3 is populated only from actual run artifacts. Improve-
ment of pair discrimination without improvement of frozen
features is reported as such. Failed or collapsed runs remain
in the record. A 20-epoch run with no numerical error does
not, by itself, establish stable representation learning or the
final ordering of methods.
6.5 Formal benchmarks and computational
comparisons
After pilot review, the planned confirmation uses CIFAR-10
and CIFAR-100, 200 pretraining epochs, and at least three
complete pretraining seeds. The protocol for retraining on
the full training set and accessing the official test set is frozen
for all methods before final evaluation. A formal 200-epoch
run restarts from the documented initialization with its own
schedule; it is not a continuation of the completed 20-epoch
cosine schedule.
The planned baseline set contains tuned RPC, SimCLR,
method-native VICReg, and SSL-HSIC; FroSSL is included
where a reliable implementation is available [1, 4, 8, 14, 17].
Matched and native variants are named separately and receive
comparable tuning opportunities. A longer training budget for
key methods tests whether a short-budget advantage persists.
Additional runs remain conditional on the previous stage’s
review, not commitments already executed.
ImageNet-100 uses a frozen, published image/class mani-
fest and a ResNet-50 backbone [5, 6]. No universal 100-class
subset is assumed. ImageNet-1K remains a possible scale
extension after measuring resource requirements. Table 4 sep-

Cross-modal and EEG tasks remain outside the current main
evaluation, rather than uncompleted empirical claims in the
abstract.
6.6 Sensitivity, ablations, and interpretation
The main stability study varies batch size, learning rate, de-
pendence strength in the controlled setting, and critic capac-
ity. The initial batch grid is {64,128,256,512}, limited to
key methods rather than a full Cartesian sweep. Learning
rates vary around independently validated method defaults.
Matched updates, seen examples, and wall-clock budgets an-
swer different questions and are labeled separately; changing
the batch at fixed epochs also changes the number of optimizer
steps.
For stochastic-gradient diagnostics, a checkpoint and its
normalization state are fixed while fresh mini-batches are sam-
pled. Absolute norms are reported together with scale-aware
summaries, and normalized ratios are not interpreted when
their denominators approach zero. An evaluation-mode gradi-
ent diagnostic is distinguished from training-mode BatchNorm
behavior. These measurements assess the ordinary update in
Eq. (17); they do not introduce a different update method.
Subsequent controlled ablations may vary tanh versus an
unrestricted critic, the number of off-diagonal partners, crit-
ic/projector capacity, and joint versus ordinary alternating
updates. Only one declared factor changes at a time. The pilot
does not optimize the CS transform or add anti-collapse terms.
Any future loss extension requires its own configuration and
component controls rather than being silently absorbed into
VCS-QMI.
The planned quantitative figures show target-specific esti-
mation error, frozen-feature accuracy versus batch/learning-
rate changes, and accuracy versus measured training time.
They must be generated from logged runs. We do not draw
illustrative performance curves or extrapolate GPU time from
hardware names. The numerical and visual conclusions are
conditional on completed evidence, not the working title.

The distinctions matter. The target is not Shannon MI,
and its quadratic functional overlaps with established relative-

8 Conclusion
We study a mixture-reference CS dependence measure with
an exact quadratic variational representation, a bounded PMI
critic, and a squared regression gap. A conventional sample-
based estimator and joint two-view SSL update connect this
construction to visual representation learning. The revised
evaluation distinguishes estimator behavior, development vali-
dation, and formal frozen-feature benchmarks. Which statisti-
cal properties translate into practical learning benefits remains
an empirical question to be answered by the specified experi-
ments.

D.2 Model and initialization
The backbone is a standard ResNet-18 initialized without
pretrained weights. Replace its first convolution by a 3 × 3,
stride-one, padding-one convolution with 64 output channels
and no bias. Replace the initial max-pool and final classifier
by identities, retaining global average pooling. The output is
h ∈ R512.
The projector consists of a bias-free 512 → 512 linear layer,
BatchNorm, ReLU, and a 512 → 128 linear layer with bias.
No final BatchNorm is used. VCS and SimCLR apply L2
normalization with norm floor 10−8 after projection. VICReg
receives the raw, unnormalized 128-dimensional output. The
2B views pass through the backbone and projector together so
that their BatchNorm policy is identical across the matched
runs.
The critic is 256 → 512 → 512 → 1, with ReLU at both
hidden layers and tanh at the output. Its input is the ordered
pair concatenation. Hidden layers retain standard linear-layer
initialization; the last weight uses Xavier uniform initialization
with gain 0.1 and bias zero. It is not initialized to an all-zero
weight matrix. Critic initialization, data loading, and pairing
use separate random streams so that an extra module does not
change the common encoder/projector initialization or image
order. Initial state-dictionary hashes are recorded.
D.3 Objective reference
The numerical computation is
J = (t_pos.mean() - t_neg.mean()
- 0.5 * t_pos.square().mean()
- 0.5 * t_neg.square().mean())
loss = -J
R = (0.5 * (1 - t_pos).square().mean()
+ 0.5 * (-1 - t_neg).square().mean())
The square is inside the sample mean. Both terms use the
same critic, and positive/negative classes retain equal total
weight. The raw J can be negative: with bounded scores its
empirical range is [−3,1]. Its nonnegativity is not enforced,
and no logarithmic CS transform is evaluated during pilot
training.
At K = 1, a concatenated balanced mean-squared error
equals R, so it differs from −J by a constant one. Multiply-
ing that loss by another half changes gradients and is not an
equivalent unrecorded implementation. Repeating an identical
negative-score list does not change its mean or the target prior.
A dedicated CPU pairing generator draws one nonzero
cyclic shift per update. Its state is checkpointed. No self-
pair, label-based exclusion, selection-set partner, or detached
negative branch is allowed. The extension to multiple shifts
uses different nonzero shifts with K ≤ B − 1, but the current
run fixes K = 1.

the labeled memory bank, and selection images as queries.
Use k = 200 and temperature 0.1 at epochs 0, 5, 10, and
20. Weight neighbors by exp((s j − smax)/0.1) and process
queries in blocks rather than materializing an unnecessarily
large similarity matrix.
For VCS critic diagnostics, all modules are in evaluation
mode. Generate two independent training-distribution aug-
mentations of each selection image and use the documented
nonzero-shift pairing. Make four augmentation/pairing re-

is merged rather than paired with itself.

development use of the selection set is acknowledged. A final

tive rank. At zero trace, record rank zero and a zero-covariance
flag rather than assigning entropy to an undefined distribution.
Only small numerical negative eigenvalues are clipped; larger
violations are recorded as numerical problems.
Before each optimizer step at the documented logging in-
terval, record module-wise raw gradient norms along with
score moments and learning rates. The pilot does not clip
these gradients. It does not equate a smaller norm with a
better direction. Later multi-batch gradient studies hold the
same model and BatchNorm state fixed, and explicitly iden-
tify whether they measure evaluation-mode or training-mode
behavior.
A saturated critic, low feature rank, or no gain over initial-
ization is a reported research outcome, not authorization to
add a new loss. A nonfinite loss/gradient, incorrect pairing, or
data-role contamination is an implementation failure requiring
a stop and diagnosis. Both categories remain visible in the
report.
E.4 Cost, randomness, and recovery
Record train time and evaluation time separately, along
with peak allocated/reserved GPU memory, model parameter
counts, optimizer steps, view counts, and pair counts. Images
per second refers to base images; views per second counts both
augmentations. A fixed-budget comparison and a fixed-time
comparison are both labeled with their actual budgets.
Checkpoints include model, optimizer, and scheduler states;
intended schedule horizon; configuration and manifest digests;
and Python, NumPy, CPU/CUDA, loader, pairing, and evalu-
ation random-generator states. Save initialization, final, and
declared intermediate checkpoints. Epoch-boundary recovery
is the default. Exact mid-epoch replay is not claimed without
restoring sampler positions, worker prefetch, and augmenta-
tion states. Evaluation does not permanently disable training
gradients or consume training randomness. A recovery test is
separate from any downstream-accuracy claim.
