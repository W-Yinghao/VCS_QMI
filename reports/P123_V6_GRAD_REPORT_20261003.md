## 1. Identities checked
- **[identity]** Actual block = the P115 training-loss decomposition in every VCS batch (max relative error 2.8e-7); for the fixed-(2, −1) runs the
  counterfactual affine (2, 0.5) equals the actual block exactly (|ΔLp| + |ΔLq| = 0).
- **[identity]** For the affine scorer, |∂A/∂s| on positives peaks at s₊ = κ − log2/(2a) and decreases above it.  So when positives sit above the peak,
  the lowest-similarity positives carry the largest scalar action.  At epoch 800 the positive median s is 0.92–0.98 against s₊ = 0.33 for (2, 0.5); the
  positive data mass within ±0.05 of the peak is ≤ 0.007 in every fixed-scale checkpoint.

## 5. Hypotheses for full-training tests (not tested here)
1. **[hypothesis]** A higher fixed κ (or any scorer that moves the positive gradient peak into the positive bulk) reduces the concentration of the update
   on low-overlap positives during training, and this matters under strong augmentation, where low-IoU positives are 29 % of pairs and already carry
   0.87–0.93 of the positive encoder gradient in the VCS runs (0.70 for SimCLR).  This connects to P112, where strong-aug (2, κ 0.75) was +0.70 over G2-strong at 3 seeds; P112 used K8 / detach, so
   it is not the same unit.
2. **[hypothesis]** The curvature λ = ±0.25 at (2, 0.5) changes the read-only low-IoU concentration much less than κ does (|Δ| ≤ 0.09 vs up to 0.23).  If the
   P126 runs differ from A-P3 in accuracy, that difference is unlikely to come mainly from low-IoU gradient allocation — another route (e.g. negative-side
   action, zero shift) would have to be examined.
3. **[hypothesis]** CIFAR-100's lower low-IoU share and higher P/Q ratio at the same similarity statistics suggest the positive term organises the update
   differently across datasets.  Whether that relates to A-P3's missing CIFAR-100 gain over the recipe is open (P124 granularity readouts are the
   complementary test).

**Stage B (P126).**  These read-only results inform the stage-B decision on the four curvature runs without deciding it.  The prereg forbids selecting a
curvature by read-only J or gradient norms, and §3 shows the λ effect on gradient allocation is small and within batch noise.
