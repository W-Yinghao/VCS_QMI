## 2. Pre-fixed readings
1. **Candidate-support adaptation works, for every objective.**  Training on the proposals that are ranked at test time lifts all-proposal
   accuracy by **+13.8 to +20.6 points** over the given-box-trained checkpoints (all intervals far from 0), from a zero-shot level of 33–43 %.
   Pre-fixed wording: this common gain is a **candidate-support adaptation result, not a VCS-only advantage**.
2. **The objective ordering does not change.**  VCS − softmax intervals lie below 0 on both datasets.  Pre-fixed wording: **"softmax leads by 1.85
   (RefCOCOg) and 3.22 (RefCOCO+) points on proposals even when all objectives are trained on them."**  The gap is larger than in Table C (−0.63 /
   −2.20), so adaptation helps softmax slightly more.
3. **Same posterior:** VCS ≈ JS on both datasets (+0.38, +0.17; intervals contain 0).
4. **Estimator view:** VCS / JS encoders still expose more critic-fittable dependence on the same p / q (J_recal ×1.07–1.08 over softmax), much less
   than the ×1.33 of the given-box setting (VL3).  As before, this does not translate into ranking accuracy.

## 3. Secondary observations (descriptive)
- **The deployable no-training arm is real:** detector-score top-K with K chosen on CAL (K = 8 for every VL3 checkpoint) raises the given-box-trained
  checkpoints by +6.3 to +13.0 points (e.g. RefCOCO+ VCS 53.5 → 66.5), and there VCS ≥ softmax on RefCOCO+ (66.5 vs 65.6).  Proposal-aware training
  is still 6.6–10.7 points better, and after it the preselection adds at most 1.0 point.  This confirms R4-VL0's exploratory lead, now with K chosen on CAL.
- **Box quality:** softmax leads more at IoU 0.75 (+3.7 / +4.3) than at 0.5.  Its top-1 boxes are also tighter.
- **Cost on given boxes:** proposal-trained encoders lose 3–6 points of given-box Top-1 relative to VL3 (2.9–5.6) (e.g. RefCOCO+ VCS 86.9 → 81.7).  The
  candidate distributions trade off.
- **Selection:** softmax peaks early (epochs 2–4), VCS / JS later (4–8), as in VL3.
- **Margins:** the ordering VCS ≈ JS < softmax stays (×100: 7.3 / 7.8 / 9.3; 7.3 / 6.9 / 12.9), consistent with R4-VL0's many-candidate reading.

## 4. Interpretation (hypothesis, not tested)
With about 45–50 candidates per image and a uniform reference ν, VCS / JS learn a per-pair density ratio in which most Q-mass sits on easy
negatives.  Softmax optimises the ranking among *all* the image's candidates directly (its log-sum-exp concentrates the gradient on the hardest
negatives), which is the deployment criterion.  A non-uniform ν (e.g. emphasising overlapping or same-category proposals) would change what the
estimator objectives emphasise.  It is a different reference law and would need a new pre-registration.

## 5. Caveats
- Two datasets, one backbone, three seeds; cells share a backbone family, so the pooled number is descriptive.
- FIT proposals come from a detector that saw the FIT images (recall 98.0–98.9 % vs DEV 97.2–98.7 %; recorded in the protocol).
- The secondary diagnostics of the new encoders (error types, oracle curves, 2×2) are being scored (`--source r4vl1`) and will be added as an addendum.

Readings (descriptive):
1. **What the adaptation fixed:** part / duplicate and unannotated-box errors fall by about two thirds (from 32–35 % to 9–11 % of queries combined),
   the failure mode R4-VL0 identified.
2. **What remains:** the dominant error is now a same-category other object (14–16 %), the classic referring problem.  Encoders also give up 3–5
   points of discrimination among the annotated referred objects (A+C), consistent with the given-box Top-1 loss.
3. **The VCS deficit is still a many-candidate effect.**  It is near zero against 1 distractor, grows with the count, and is largest against all
   proposals.  VCS keeps the smallest margins (on RefCOCO+ less than 60 % of softmax's) and makes more part / duplicate errors than softmax.  This
   is the regime the uniform ν under-weights, which motivates the drafted R4-VL2 (detector-score ν).
