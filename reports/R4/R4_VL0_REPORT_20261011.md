## 2. Readings (controlled diagnostics, not a causal decomposition)
1. **Box quality is not the bottleneck.**  Replacing the annotated target box by the best detected box costs 0.6–1.0 point for every objective (A → B).
2. **Distractor type at matched count is not the bottleneck either.**  With as many detected distractors as annotated ones, accuracy is *higher* than
   with the annotated same-scene objects (A+D 89–90 vs A+C 85–87).  In that setting **VCS ranks better than softmax** on both datasets (+0.6 to
   +1.0, intervals exclude 0).
3. **The number of candidates is.**  Accuracy falls steadily from 1 to all ≈ 44–52 distractors (oracle curve), and softmax degrades more slowly.  On
   RefCOCO+, VCS leads at 1–3 distractors and trails with all of them.  The softmax-trained encoders keep a larger margin between the best valid and
   best invalid proposal (≈ 1.1 × 10⁻² cosine more), which is what an extreme-value contest over many candidates rewards.
4. **What wins the contest:** a part or duplicate box of the target (23–25 % of queries), then same-category other objects (7–9 %).  Training only
   ever showed whole referred objects as candidates.
5. **Deployable lead (exploratory):** ranking only the top-8 proposals by detector score (query-independent) raises every objective by 7–13
   points over all proposals, despite lower coverage.  At K = 4–8, VCS ≥ softmax on RefCOCO+.  K was read on DEV here, so a deployable claim needs
   K chosen on CAL in a new protocol.

## 3. Consequence for R4-VL1 (proposal-aware fine-tuning)
- The diagnostics point to the *count / extreme-value* regime with part / duplicate boxes, not to box quality.  Training with all FIT proposals
  under the proposal-reference Q_ν (docs/04 §4) targets exactly that, for all three objectives alike.
- A cheaper, deployable comparison arm follows from item 5: detector-score top-K preselection with K fixed on CAL, applied to every objective, with
  no training.  Proposed as part of the VL1 protocol, not run.
