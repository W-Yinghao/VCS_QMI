- Both CS-route alternatives end in **dimensional collapse of h** (effective rank 10–39 vs 146–150 for VCS-N), which accounts for the large kNN deficits.
- S-Kernel reaches a high J (0.91–0.92) with a larger actual gate than VCS-N (0.17–0.19 vs 0.12) and 10× larger projector gradients, yet puts the
  representation in a few directions: a flexible read-out on fixed random features can separate P from Q without the encoder spreading h.  The cosine
  critic, which can only read an inner product, forces the encoder to organise the whole sphere.
- CS-K-native pulls positive pairs to cos ≈ 1.0 and spreads negatives around −0.08 on z, but h collapses to ≈ 10–15 dimensions; the plug-in statistic
  is satisfied by a low-dimensional embedding (P86 §7: at this dimension the classical statistic is bandwidth-dominated).

## 3. What can be written
- Can: inside the same SSL protocol and budget, replacing the neural cosine critic by a kernel-feature critic of the same J lowers linear accuracy by 2.1
  and kNN by 5.1; training with the classical kernel CS-QMI objective lowers them by 8.5 and 13.7.  Both kernel routes collapse h dimensionally.
- Together with P86 (estimation: VCS-N lower same-target error than every kernel estimator), the direct CS comparison favours the neural variational route
  on both the estimation and the representation-learning side.  This does not change the controls' lead (tuned SimCLR 88.32 at 8×).
- Cannot: attribute the Table-A gap to the estimator alone — the function class of the critic differs (inner-product vs random-feature read-out), which
  is the point of the table, and the kernel bandwidth / m were selected on a 1× dev grid only.
