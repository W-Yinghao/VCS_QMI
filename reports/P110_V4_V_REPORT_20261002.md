## 1. V1 — label efficiency (CIFAR-10) and frozen transfer (CIFAR-100)
All values are selection-split top-1 from the probe chosen inside FIT, given as mean ± sd over 3 seeds × draws. The recipe's fixed probe is in the results file: it agrees within ±0.4 on CIFAR-10, but on CIFAR-100 it reads up to 2.6 points lower (largest for SimCLR and U2), so the FIT-selected probe is the one reported.

| labels | G2 | U2 | recipe VCS | SimCLR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CIFAR-10, 1 % | **84.97 ± 0.30** | 83.14 ± 0.64 | 80.39 ± 0.47 | 83.92 ± 0.95 |
| CIFAR-10, 10 % | **87.79 ± 0.31** | 86.39 ± 0.24 | 85.41 ± 0.30 | 87.07 ± 0.49 |

| CIFAR-100 transfer, 100 % | 47.63 ± 0.84 | 45.73 ± 0.54 | **52.70 ± 0.48** | 38.15 ± 0.41 |

The reading is descriptive. There is no threshold, and "not different" is not claimed.

- **CIFAR-10, G2 against SimCLR.** G2 leads at every label fraction, and the lead grows as labels get scarcer: +1.05 at 1 %, +0.72 at 10 %, +0.38 at 100 %.
- **CIFAR-10, G2 against recipe VCS.** G2 leads by +4.58 at 1 % and +1.44 at 100 %.
- **CIFAR-10, U2.** U2 sits between recipe VCS and SimCLR.
- **The 100 % column checks out.** It reproduces the training-time linear numbers: G2 88.70, SimCLR 88.32, recipe 87.01.
- **CIFAR-100 transfer reverses the CIFAR-10 order.** Recipe VCS transfers best (52.70 at 100 %), then G2 (47.63), U2 (45.73) and SimCLR (38.15, −14.5 below recipe VCS).
  - *Hypothesis, not tested here:* this fits the I1 finding (P109) that the recipe representation keeps more low-level image information, such as colour, in h, which would not help on CIFAR-10 but carry over to the new label set.
  - **Probe caveat (main session, P98):** P98 found that this linear probe on standardised h is probe-limited for SimCLR's 8× features on CIFAR-100 — under a normalised / longer probe the VCS-vs-SimCLR ordering was not robust. SimCLR's 38.15 may therefore be partly a probe effect; no kNN or robust-probe transfer readout was run. **The SimCLR-vs-VCS transfer gap is not claimed;** the comparison among the three VCS-family encoders (same feature geometry) is the readable part.
  - This is a frozen readout on transfer. It is separate from P91's from-scratch CIFAR-100 SSL and is not mixed with it.

## 2. V2 — corruption and compression tolerance (development phase)
The head is the recipe probe trained on clean FIT h. Corruptions are applied to the 5 000 selection images: 6 families × 5 severities.
- **Table values:** mean ± sd over 3 seeds.
- **Per-encoder CIs:** clustered by image. They are about ±0.6 on mCA and about ±0.5 on relative drop, and are in the unit files.
- **Comparison:** absolute accuracy and relative drop are both reported; neither is picked as the primary measure after the fact.

| family | clean | mCA | mean relative drop % | consistency % | Gaussian noise | blur | JPEG | pixelate | contrast | brightness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G2 | 88.70 | 63.50 ± 0.54 | 28.41 ± 0.44 | 67.13 | 34.21 | 57.82 | 68.12 | 47.32 | 86.84 | 86.69 |
| U2 | 87.59 | **65.09 ± 0.55** | **25.69 ± 0.63** | **69.00** | **40.50** | **60.10** | 69.11 | 49.16 | 86.13 | 85.54 |
| recipe VCS | 87.02 | 60.26 ± 0.23 | 30.75 ± 0.59 | 63.85 | 29.65 | 52.40 | 64.91 | 46.35 | 84.02 | 84.22 |
| SimCLR | 88.33 | 63.77 ± 0.46 | 27.80 ± 0.40 | 68.03 | 33.76 | 57.55 | **69.36** | 49.12 | 86.45 | 86.36 |

- **G2 and SimCLR are on par:** mCA 63.50 vs 63.77, relative drop 28.4 vs 27.8 %.
- **U2 is the most corruption-tolerant** on both the absolute and the relative measure: mCA +1.3 over SimCLR, relative drop −2.1 points.
  - The difference comes mostly from Gaussian noise (+6.7) and blur (+2.6).
  - This holds even though U2's clean accuracy is lower.
- **Recipe VCS is the least tolerant** (mCA 60.26, drop 30.8 %), consistent with it retaining more image-level detail.
- These are development-phase corruptions only. The standard CIFAR-10-C benchmark is not run, because it needs the owner's authorisation and is reporting-only.

## 3. V3 — does a conditional-dependence audit improve model selection? (controlled)
**Pre-stated reading.**
- Δ_k = reversal-test accuracy(B_k) − accuracy(A).
- Pooled over 4 encoder families × 3 seeds × 2 cues (colour, tag) × 2 ρ × 5 replicates.
- 95 % bootstrap CI, resampling encoder runs.
- "Improves" requires the CI to exclude 0 on the positive side **and** Δ_k to exceed the random-eligible baseline (C − A).

| rule | Δ vs A (points) | 95 % CI |
|---|---|---|
| B, VCS closed-form audit | −0.43 | [−0.58, −0.28] |
| B, exact-JS audit | −0.42 | [−0.59, −0.27] |
| B, class-conditional HSIC audit | −0.25 | [−0.38, −0.11] |
| C, random eligible member | −0.51 | [−0.67, −0.35] |

- **Verdict: no improvement shown.** Every audit-assisted rule selects members whose reversal-test accuracy is *lower* than that of rule A (highest clean-validation accuracy). The CIs lie entirely below 0.
- The audits do beat a random eligible member, by 0.08–0.26 points, so they carry some information about shortcut use. But clean-validation accuracy alone already picks a better member.
- The gate's development note anticipated this outcome: on this pool, clean-validation accuracy and reversal accuracy are positively related, which leaves little room for an audit to help.
- VCS, JS and HSIC audits are described, not ranked. HSIC is the least negative.
- Per-cell results, including worst-group accuracy and the pool-best ceiling (87.0–90.3), are in the results file.

**V3 calibration disclosure (from the freeze).** The cue construction and the class-aware audit features were fixed on gate runs that used P35 seed 0 and G2 seed 0. Both are also evaluation encoders here. The calibration did not compare rules A and B on any reversal outcome. The evaluation used fresh replicate seeds and the frozen rules.
