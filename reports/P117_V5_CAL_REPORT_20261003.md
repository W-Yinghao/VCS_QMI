## 1. Readings (frozen rules)
1. **"Calibration repairs most of the error" (selected posterior MSE ≤ 0.5 × identity): met in 0 of 12 groups.**  Best ratio 0.73 (C2 N 16384 JS: 0.142 →
   0.104); typical 0.95–0.99.  The selected calibrator is mostly latent-affine; bins are never better than latent-affine by more than noise.
2. **"The score has lost most of the dependence" (A share ≥ 0.5): all 12 groups.**  A share 0.93–0.99 except C2 N 16384 (0.72 JS, 0.79 VCS).  The fixable share
   (B_identity − B_selected)/(A + B_identity) is 0.00–0.06, except C2 N 16384 (0.26 JS, 0.19 VCS).  Decomposition QC: residual ≤ 4.4e-5 at the finest bins.
3. **End-to-end at equal total sample budget: the calibrated arm is not better.**  Spending CAL samples on fitting the critic (identity on FIT ∪ CAL) gives lower
   posterior MSE in 11 of 12 groups (paired Δ = calibrated − identity from +0.0003 to +0.127; the one negative, C2 N 16384 JS −0.0037 ± 0.0040, is within SE).
   No sample-efficiency gain is claimed.
4. **By the pre-stated rule 4 the result stands as "calibration does not help": the fitted critics' error is almost entirely dependence already lost by the
   score (A), not output mis-calibration (B).**  The next step is the critic function class / representation of (x, y), not further monotone output maps
   (no new calibrator families are added).

## 2. What this explains about P108
P108 found that Ĵ(T) reads S worse than the squared plug-in for fitted critics.  P117 shows why calibration is not the lever: with A ≈ 95 % of S − J(T),
the fitted T has lost most of the dependence information; a calibrated output cannot recover it.  The case where calibration matters somewhat (C2 at N 16384,
the high-dimensional padded condition where the fit just starts to work) is where the raw output is badly scaled (B_identity 0.03–0.04) — there latent-affine
removes most of B (fixable share ≈ 0.2).
